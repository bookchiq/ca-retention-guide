## Dissents from denial of review

The Supreme Court decides most petitions without an opinion. Justices sometimes record a dissent from the decision not to review a case. These votes are not in opinion text, so this guide quotes a third-party count rather than measuring them: for the 2024–25 term, David S. Ettinger's *At the Lectern (Horvitz & Levy)* counts 63 dissenting votes by Justice Evans (63 in criminal cases, 0 in civil cases). Source: ["The 2024–2025 term in numbers — the conferences [Updated]"](https://www.horvitzlevy.com/the-2024-2025-term-in-numbers-the-conferences-updated/), 2025-09-15.

## Limitations

California courts apply federal search-and-seizure rules, so differences between justices show mostly in how they apply precedent to close facts. Court of Appeal cases are decided by three-justice panels, so outcomes reflect the panel; authored and separate opinions are the clearest individual signals. Which cases are appealed, and who writes, are not random. Unpublished opinions are included because they are most of the record. This guide describes; it does not recommend a vote.
