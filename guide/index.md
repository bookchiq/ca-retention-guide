# California appellate justices on the November 3, 2026 ballot

*An experiment by Sarah Lewis. It is a work in progress: the [methodology](methodology.md) explains exactly how it was made, and all the [code and data](https://github.com/bookchiq/ca-retention-guide) are public, so you can judge it for yourself.*

On November 3, California voters decide whether each Supreme Court and Court of Appeal justice on the ballot keeps their seat. The ballot asks a yes-or-no question for each justice and says little else about them. This guide collects what each justice has done on the bench, with links to the court opinions, so you can look for yourself. It does not grade, rank, or recommend a vote.

## What's on each justice's page

- **Background:** who appointed them, what they did before, how earlier retention votes went, and any public discipline. Each fact links to its source.
- **Record at a glance:** how many opinions they wrote, how often those were published, and how often they wrote dissents or concurrences. Each rate is shown next to the same rate for their court.
- **Other topics:** counts for 15 more topics, from interrogation and resentencing to employment, consumer protection, and land use. Each topic's count was checked for accuracy before it was shown.
- **Case lists on four topics** (search and seizure, interrogation, jury selection, and the Racial Justice Act), for every justice with an appellate record: the cases chosen by the same fixed rules for every justice, each with a short summary and a link to the opinion. AI reviewers did the reading. Each case was read, then re-checked against the opinion text by a separate reviewer, a random 15% were checked a third time, and the author checked a random sample of the summaries.

## Before you draw conclusions

- **Court of Appeal cases are decided by panels of three.** An outcome reflects the panel. The opinions a justice wrote, and their dissents and concurrences, are the clearest signs of their own views.
- **California follows federal search-and-seizure rules.** Differences between justices show up mostly in close calls, not in the rules themselves.
- **The counts come from software that reads the opinions.** Its error rates were measured by hand and are published in the [methodology](methodology.md#validation).
- **The record starts in 2020.** The 2020 data is incomplete, so 2020 counts are low for everyone. Some justices are too new to have much of a record, and their pages say so.

If you find an error, please open an [issue](https://github.com/bookchiq/ca-retention-guide/issues) or a [pull request](https://github.com/bookchiq/ca-retention-guide/pulls) on GitHub, or email Sarah Lewis at sarah@happyhumans.com.
